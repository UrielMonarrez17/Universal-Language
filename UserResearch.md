# Alterlife — User Research

## Question, participants, and method

How can beginners participate in a French restaurant encounter while learning VR controls and recovering from comprehension difficulty?

The team supplied retrospective accounts of hands-on sessions with **Andrea Monarrez** and **Lalo Molinar**. Participants used the prototype and made spontaneous comments observed by the team. No separate pre-design interviews, survey, recording, standardized task script, or timing log was supplied.

Age, French proficiency, prior VR experience, recruitment, dates, and session duration were not provided. Target-audience fit therefore needs confirmation. Confirm consent before publishing names and quotations.

## Findings

| Participant | Reported evidence | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Andrea | “wow” | Positive initial reaction, insufficient to establish overall satisfaction or learning. |
| Andrea | “como se que esta diciendo?” — “How do I know what he is saying?” | Need for comprehension support. |
| Andrea | “necesito ayuda para aprender a utilizar el zorro compañia” — “I need help learning how to use the companion fox.” | Help itself needs explanation. |
| Andrea | Ordering friction; she did not understand why the waiter did not proceed when she omitted thanks | Unclear conversational and progression requirements. |
| Lalo | “no entiendo lo que dice” — “I don't understand what he is saying,” followed by laughter | Difficulty understanding French. Laughter alone does not establish satisfaction. |
| Lalo | Requested other countries | Interest in broader content. |
| Lalo | Tried to explore the scene and objected to restaurant-only movement | Spatial appearance created expectations of exploration. |

## User needs

Learners need to discover controls before conversation, understand the fox, recover the meaning of French prompts, hear pronunciation examples, understand stalled progression, explore within clear boundaries, and recognize available destinations.

These needs are inferred from two sessions. The broader motivation about travel access preceded the testing and was not established by this sample.

## Customer journey

This retrospective journey combines the designed route with reported observations. Unobserved thoughts and feelings are labeled as assumptions.

| Stage | Action / touchpoint | Thought | Emotion or evidence | Friction | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enter | Put on headset and inspect controls | How do I interact? | Uncertainty inferred from instruction needs | New input method | Initial controller tutorial, reported added |
| Choose country | Rotate globe and select France | Can I visit other countries? | Curiosity inferred from Lalo's request | Limited content | Available and future options |
| Choose chapter | Open book and select restaurant | What will I practise? | Anticipation is a design assumption | Understanding difficulty | Chapter illustrations and ratings |
| Meet waiter | Listen in Paris | How do I know what he is saying? | Andrea's initial enthusiasm and both testers' comprehension difficulty | Unfamiliar speech | Subtitles and repeatable dialogue |
| Seek help | Summon fox | How do I use the companion? | Andrea explicitly requested instruction | Help discovery | Tutorial before Paris, reported added |
| Order | Read menu and respond | Why is the waiter waiting? | Confusion reported for Andrea | Hidden politeness requirement | Explicit feedback requiring retesting |
| Explore | Walk around Paris | Can I go beyond the restaurant? | Lalo attempted broader exploration | Movement restriction | Expanded movement, reported implemented |
| Finish | Review and repeat | What should I improve? | Design assumption; completion not recorded | Interpreting results | Milestone and assistance information |

## Influence and limitations

The team linked Andrea's feedback to controller and companion onboarding, and Lalo's behavior to expanded movement. Subtitles and pronunciation assistance are consistent with these needs and later development requests. No follow-up participant results were provided, so improved comprehension or success cannot yet be claimed.

Future research should record participant background, dates, consent, task outcomes, and whether users independently recover from the missing-thanks condition. More participants are needed before generalizing.
