# Alterlife — Design Justification

## Design intent

Alterlife connects French communication to the practical goal of ordering in a restaurant. A spatial globe and book introduce the encounter. The supplied Figma concept informed navigation and aesthetics.

## HCI concepts and human factors

| Concept | Design application | User evidence and rationale | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition rather than recall | Controller and fox introductions | Andrea needed help learning the companion. Visible instructions make actions easier to discover. | Final onboarding is team-reported and has not been retested in the supplied record. |
| Cognitive load and progressive disclosure | Introduce controls, then help, then one conversational objective at a time | Andrea faced unfamiliar French and VR interaction together. Staging these demands can reduce simultaneous learning requirements. | Tutorials should remain short and understandable. |
| Visibility of system status and feedback | Listening status, dialogue, objectives, subtitles, milestone feedback | Both testers struggled to understand the waiter. Andrea did not understand why progression stopped. | The missing-thanks requirement needs explicit feedback and a targeted retest. |
| Mapping and familiar objects | Turn a globe, open a book, inspect a menu | Familiar actions give navigation and conversation a meaningful context. | Familiar objects still require controller instruction. |
| User control and freedom | Wrist navigation, replay, help, and expanded movement | Lalo attempted to explore beyond the restaurant. The team expanded movement in response. | Clear boundaries and a route back to the task remain necessary. |
| Constraints and recovery | Confirm playable content only; model pronunciation through help | Unavailable chapters cannot break the route. Help supports recovery while preserving the learner's speaking turn. | Bounded recognition can reject valid paraphrases. |

The table includes cognition and interaction principles. Each member should connect the terminology to concepts actually covered in class during the defense.

## Evidence-led decisions

Andrea's observations led to initial controller guidance and a companion explanation before Paris. Both participants' comprehension difficulty supports subtitles and contextual help. Lalo's exploration attempt led to broader movement. His request for more countries remains future scope.

Andrea's confusion after omitting thanks is a specific unresolved verification question. The dialogue should communicate what is expected and why the waiter is waiting. Onboarding alone does not establish that this problem is solved.

Changing help cards from automatic answers to pronunciation examples followed a later team request. It supports speaking practice, but should not be attributed to a participant comment that was never reported.

## VR comfort and accessibility

Head tracking, pointing, direct manipulation, teleportation, and snap turns fit the spatial setting. Text complements speech. Posture and movement options accommodate preferences. Readable text, reachable controls, collisions, and comfort still require headset checks. Two informal sessions cannot establish accessibility for specific disabilities or absence of motion sickness.
